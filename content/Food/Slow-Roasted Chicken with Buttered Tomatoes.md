Recipe from Nothing Fancy by Alison Roman

![[Attachments/C70E4102-FE98-4A5A-A172-F2A620367560_1_105_c.jpeg|center|363]]

I don't know why it took me so long to make the [recipe](https://www.alisoneroman.com/recipes/slow-roasted-oregano-chicken-with-buttered-tomatoes/) from the cover of Nothing Fancy. After the roaring success of the Father's Day [[Low and Slow Rib Roast with Rosemary and Anchovy|rib roast]], I was ready to dive back into the Roman-verse, this time with a quicker and (much) cheaper roast chicken. The defining feature of this recipe is the slow roast at 325F for 2.5 - 3 hours, which "gives you golden skin, tender meat and plenty of salty, savory chicken juices to serve as a sauce." For reference, my favorite roast chicken recipe (which has never failed to lead to golden skin and tender meat BTW) is Melissa Clark's take on pollo a la brasa [Peruvian Roasted Chicken with Spicy Cilantro Sauce](https://cooking.nytimes.com/recipes/1018974-peruvian-roasted-chicken-with-spicy-cilantro-sauce?smid=share-url), which includes a wet marinade and a 35 - 45 minute cook time at 450F.

Alison's recipe only calls for salt, pepper, oregano and fennel seeds as the seasoning. Nothing fancy, indeed. The chicken roasts in the baking dish with some tomatoes, roasted garlic and butter, which serves as the sauce for the chicken.

I bought the smallest whole chicken I could find at Vons, which turned out to be a little over 4 pounds. I seasoned the chicken with salt and pepper about 5 hours before cooking. The original recipe calls for a bunch of oregano, but I subbed thyme since oregano wasn't available. This proved a little tricky when I had to pluck and sprinkle tiny thyme leaves over the chicken, but I wouldn't omit the fresh herbs.

Some comments say that the cook time is too long and led to dried-out chicken. I decided to roast the chicken at 300F instead, and set a timer to check in at 2.5 hours. To my surprise, the chicken was done right at the 2.5 hour mark, despite the lower temperature. If I had roasted it for longer or set the oven to the original 325F, I'm sure it would've dried out. My garlic was starting to get on the burnt side of roasted, but the tomatoes didn't burst and were kept more or less intact.

I need to play around with this recipe more and find the balance between a jammy confit garlic and silky butter sauce. I totally forgot to add the 2 tbsp of red wine vinegar that Alison calls for, and I was reaching for the salt in the end to add a little more seasoning to the dish. Ultimately, it was pretty good because roast chickens are pretty good in general. In principle, I like the simplicity of this recipe and agree that it doesn't take much to create a satisfying, home-y meal. Unfortunately, this doesn't come close to the Peruvian chicken recipe. I just think the soy sauce, chili paste and lime juice in the wet marinade pack a much bigger punch than salt and pepper. I'm willing to try this again with some more tweaks.
