Recipe by Paola Briseño-González from NYT Cooking
![[Attachments/PXL_20261105_033523507.jpg|center|363]]
To say that I've been in a cooking rut lately is an understatement. Life and family stuff have gotten in the way, and I've fallen back into freezer meals and scrounging around the pantry for (unsightly) food combinations. The morning of my grandmother's funeral I went to brunch with Chris and his sister. This brunch was mainly to walk through the motions of River-sitting, since they were taking care of him while we went out of town the weekend after. We went to Cocina de Barrio in Encinitas, which I thought was a relatively new location but turns out they've been open for over a year! Things move quickly when you don't live in town for a few years...

Cocina de Barrio had a special for enchiladas Suizas - I'm not usually an enchilada person, but they seemed hearty enough for a dull and dreary day. I did feel stuffed after, but it was almost a comfort for the hours ahead. Two weeks later in a moment of recipe analysis paralysis, I came across this [recipe](https://cooking.nytimes.com/recipes/1027011-enchiladas-suizas-creamy-chicken-enchiladas?smid=share-url) for enchiladas Suizas in NYT Cooking. The recipe was approachable while I still felt the satisfaction of taking on a slightly longer 'project'.

All of the ingredients were easily sourced at Vons, including the pound of tomatillos. (I remember my mom came home from the 99 Cents store once with a bag full of tomatillos. We both had no idea what to do with them, I think we ended up making a soup? We should have made enchiladas instead.) I ended up using boneless skinless chicken breasts for the chicken, the resulting broth was still flavorful although I ended up adding some chicken bouillon powder when blending the salsa together.

The trickiest part of the recipe was frying the tortillas. It took a few tortillas to find the correct mix of firm, yet pliable. In the end, I'm not sure if frying the tortillas even mattered at all. The recipe photo did NOT resemble what I ended up making... Everything got bathed in salsa at the end, and there wasn't any crispiness to the enchilada. I didn't even see a use for the fried tortilla when rolling it up with the chicken mix.

I ended up baking the enchiladas in the dutch oven. I just managed to squeeze the 12th tortilla in there before pouring the salsa on top and scattering a ridiculous amount of cheese on top. 10 minutes in the oven was enough for a melty, gooey layer to form. The enchiladas were delicious. The final step of the recipe involves scattering half of a thinly sliced onion on top. I was initially skeptical about this since it seemed like _too much_ raw onion, but it ended up adding a more varied texture to a dish which could (generously) be considered mushy. The serrano chiles added a subtle kick that elevated the enchiladas from a pure creamy/fatty meal to something with slightly more...complexity? The salsa reminded me of my beloved That Green Sauce from HEB, which I miss very much. My dad doesn't enjoy heavy, cheesy dishes but ended up giving the enchiladas two thumbs up.

My main complaint with this recipe is the time and DISHES that it requires. A tip at the bottom mentions a swap for leftover and/or rotisserie chicken. I'd consider using leftover chicken before doing any of the suggested swaps in the comments, like using canned tomatillos or (gasp!) jarred enchilada sauce. I couldve shaved off at least 30 minutes of cook time, after poaching, cooling and shredding the chicken. From start to finish, the enchiladas took 90 minutes to cook and yielded ~5 portions. It was a crowd pleaser in the house, but be cautious of all the prep before starting a recipe on a weekday night.
