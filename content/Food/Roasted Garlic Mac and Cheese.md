Recipe by Dan Pelosi from NYT Cooking
![[Attachments/E8C94223-E15D-42D3-93F7-BE488510E4A7_1_105_c.jpeg|center|363]]
Happy (almost) Thanksgiving! I should say that I didn't even make this mac and cheese for Thanksgiving, I only made it because I wanted mac and cheese. I'm caught up in the spirit of Thanksgiving though, so I decided it was time to share my thoughts on this [recipe](https://cooking.nytimes.com/recipes/791922537-roasted-garlic-mac-and-cheese?smid=share-url) I made a few weeks ago.

Sometimes you just want a homemade mac and cheese. I don't have a lot of nostalgia for Kraft mac, or even Annie's white cheddar shells for that matter. It just wasn't something I ate at home, but I welcomed it whenever I was at a friend's house. However, homemade mac is something else entirely. I love breadcrumbs, baked mac and cheese with a golden crust, and ideally some crunchy noodle bits for a delightful texture. Unfortunately, I've learned that there's a _mac and cheese gap_ in my relationship. Chris and I have conflicting ideas of what the ideal mac and cheese is, and everything I like in my mac, he does NOT!!!

I ended up winning out when Chris selected this recipe. (I wanted to make the [Tini mac and cheese](https://cooking.nytimes.com/recipes/793042565-tini-younger-mac-and-cheese?smid=share-url), but alas, another time.) I ended up using cavatappi for the noodles, elbow pasta is so boring. Live a little! The required 4 heads of garlic is a bit extra, especially considering the time it takes to roast the garlic, wait for it to cool, and then squeeze the garlics out of their skins to form a paste. Roasting the garlic for 1 hour at 425F did the trick, although there were some charred bits here and there. I was a smidge impatient while waiting for the garlic to cool. It was late, I was hungry. My garlic didn't pop out from the skin consistently, and by the end of the process my hands were sticky and covered in garlic skin. In the end, I probably ended up with 3 heads worth of roasted garlic. It wasn't a problem, as the garlic flavor still came through, especially as the mac was baking in the oven. But still, it's not as simple in execution as it seems.

The rest of the recipe was straightforward. The roux came together nicely, and the cheeses melted perfectly. I used a truffle gruyere, which was fine. Just use regular gruyere and save yourself a dollar. In the effort to reduce the amount of dishes I had to do, I decided to bake the mac in the same dutch oven that I had used to melt the cheese and mix the noodles in, instead of transferring to a greased baking dish. I would later find out this was not the right move. The mac and cheese clung to the ENTIRE bottom of the pan and required a lot of elbow grease to wash out. I really do love my dutch oven, but I wouldn't say it's mac and cheese-friendly. (It works wonders for [[Enchiladas Suizas|enchiladas]], though!)

After 30 minutes in the oven at 400F, the mac and cheese turned out wonderfully. The grated Parmesan didn't do much for me, but the gruyere added a nice funk. We had plenty of leftovers the next day, but mid-day mac and cheese is such an indulgence that I felt better eating it at dinner. Overall, I think any mac and cheese fan would enjoy this recipe. The roasted garlic fancies up the dish, and you wouldn't be able to replicate the flavors without the garlic paste. Served the mac with some frozen broccoli to add the semblance of health.
