Recipe by David Tanis from NYT Cooking

![[Attachments/E5BC2A59-B6EA-4D77-B72F-E55804931C6A_1_102_o.jpeg|center|363]]

I'm not much of a baker, I consider it too precise and almost...inorganic of an art compared to cooking any other recipes that I've written about. Of course, this could just be cope, because I'm too lazy to source different various baking ingredients and accoutrements. The ingredients I do have tend to expire because once again, I don't bake. And thus, the cycle continues.

Last week, in an effort to get out of my [[Enchiladas Suizas|funk]], I made the Alison Roman [chickpea Stew](https://www.alisoneroman.com/recipes/spiced-chickpea-stew-with-coconut-and-turmeric/). Its infamy (chana masala ripoff? you be the judge!) is sort of charming to me now, in the post-woke 1.0 era. Anyways, the recipe is easy enough, it's a chickpea and coconut stew that Alison suggests should be served with pita. Since the stew is simple and mostly involves premade canned ingredients, I figured that attempting homemade pita would be the perfect project. The NYT Cooking [recipe](https://cooking.nytimes.com/recipes/1016071-homemade-pita-bread?smid=share-url) from David Tanis has 5 stars and a LOT of puff discourse in the comments, so what else could I ask for? I was only cooking for myself and Chris, but I told myself that Chris would be able to taste it if my meal was _too_ low effort. Obviously I was being delusional, and there were other things on my mind that I needed a distraction from.

Fast forward one week, and I've made this recipe three times. It's great! Unfortunately for me, I've substituted various ingredients based off of pantry availability that I haven't consistently repeated it:

1. Version 1: fresh yeast, (expired?) Bob's Red Mill AP flour, expired Bob's Red Mill whole wheat flour
2. Version 2: yeast sliiightly past its best by date (August 2026), expired 365 AP flour since I finished the Bob's AP, expired Bob's Red Mill WW
3. Version 3: back to fresh yeast, expired 365 flour, expired Bob's Red Mill WW

Please believe me when I say that there was nothing obviously wrong with the flour...it smelled fine, didn't make me sick, and each end result tasted fresh and fluffy. If anything, this could be a testament to my baking that my pita turned out wonderfully even though my flour wasn't in an ideal state? I also cooked the first batch of pita on a cast iron grill pan. After reading how much back-and-forth the recipe involved in cooking an individual pita for 2 minutes, flipping it to cook for another minute, and repeating for 7 more times, I decided to just preheat the pan at 475 F and cook the pita on the stovetop. The pita had a delightful chew to it, thanks to either the fresh yeast or Bob's Red Mill AP flour, but it didn't puff to a signature pillowy pita texture. (The photo above is V1.)

When attempting the recipe for the second time, I unearthed a packet of yeast that I'd bought earlier this year and subsequently forgot about. I figured I might as well use it up, but the dough mixture rose considerably less than versions 1 and 3. I also decided to use a larger baking sheet to cook the pita in the oven for Version 2, as I could fit a few pitas on a sheet at the same time. Chris helped me this time, and the joint effort meant that the pitas were consistently done. Chris rolled the pitas while I arranged them on the sheet. This time, they puffed! I was thrilled that my pitas resembled the photos on r/baking. The pitas were light and fluffy, but didn't have as much chew as version 2. They had a rougher texture, similar to a grocery store pita. Unfortunately for my puffs, this was probably my least favorite pita iteration.

A few days later, I made Alison Roman's chicken tikka masala (this is unironically good to my uncultured palate, but I feel like a caricature with how many Alison recipes I've documented so far) and had recently bought some hummus from the farmers market. Another excuse to make pita! After the slight texture disappointment with version 2, I went back to the fresh yeast. Version 3 resulted in the best dough that I'd made so far. I left the mix to rise for about 90 minutes compared to 60 minutes that the recipe calls for. I wish I could say this was unintentional, but I was lazy. I was on my own when baking the pita this time, and I lost some time when moving between the oven and rolling out the dough balls. There was more puff compared to version 1, but less than version 2. Whereas my pitas in version 2 puffed and pillowed completely, my pitas in version 3 bubbled up in pockets.

Version 3 turned out to be my favorite pitas, although Chris prefers Version 1. I assume that the biggest quality factor is the fresh yeast, although I didn't know that slightly old yeast could make such a difference. The pitas from Version 3 also held up the best, tasting fresh and fluffy the day after. Ultimately I'm glad that I embarked on this pita journey, and I had no idea that I'd make this much pita over the course of a week. They tasted significantly better than anything I've gotten from a grocery store OR farmers market. I can't recall the last time I've had pita this good, expired flour or not. I still have a mountain of flour left, so next step...naan?
