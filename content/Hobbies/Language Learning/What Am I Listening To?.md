Here's a brief guide to my favorite sources for Spanish comprehensible input. For a more in-depth look at my language learning journey, see [[My Thoughts on Dreaming Spanish After Reaching Level 6]].

Beginner (CI content only):

- [Dreaming Spanish](https://www.dreaming.com)
  - The GOAT of beginner-level comprehensible input. I continue to watch DS every day and will continue to pay for a long time. There are a wide range of topics, and you're sure to find something interesting. I mostly watch the intermediate and advanced content now, but the beginner and super beginner videos are where DS really shines.
  - I pay for the premium version for \$8/month, which is more than worth it. There's an extra charge if you want to bundle Dreaming Spanish with Dreaming French, which I haven't tried yet.
  - Some of my favorite videos and series on DS - these are all non-paywalled:
    - [Michelle bakes a Barbie princess cake](https://app.dreaming.com/spanish/watch?id=657333c685c9475147128187)
    - Shel and Agustina play [Keep Talking and Nobody Explodes](https://app.dreaming.com/spanish/watch?id=6628e355af0b443dc42fd5ff)
    - [Cities of Colombia](https://app.dreaming.com/spanish/watch?id=67974cba46d8c201f7850291)
- [Cuéntame!](https://open.spotify.com/show/5rNQFzsH92cZpwLDSbUmmc)
  - A slow-paced beginner podcast. Marta speaks very clearly and the topics are interesting enough. I quickly outgrew this, but this was the first podcast that was 90 - 95% accessible to me.

Intermediate (mix of learner and easier "native" content):

- [Spanish Boost Gaming](https://www.youtube.com/@spanishboostgaming)
  - I'm not one to watch video game playthroughs in English, but Martin is super engaging and there are occasional references to Dreaming Spanish. I find the structure of a playthrough is helpful for language learning, and Martin always stops to explain any tricky dialogue. My favorite series are Supermarket Simulator, Waterpark Simulator and Schedule 1.
- [Juega con Juarez](https://www.youtube.com/@JuegaConJuarez)
  - More video game playthroughs. Juarez's playthrough of [Mineko's Night Market](https://youtube.com/playlist?list=PLs-AikOzcAJEeTapzptbzbNztnvx7O3wb\&si=luNWZAYpdx21TvzL) got me through the intermediate slump. His series reading of [Tintin: Destination Moon](https://youtube.com/playlist?list=PLs-AikOzcAJEGT_DSIv5X08Vc6oc_YT05\&si=ieq5JfpHlJ0-lANL) is also a delight, and very nostalgic as a Tintin lover.
- [Español al Vuelo](https://open.spotify.com/show/59p2WSqYj7jJPcr1kml9vF?si=fa85623142df4895)
  - Franco is such a skilled storyteller who can still be hilarious while creating intermediate Spanish content. I'd say his Argentine accent is stronger than Martin's, but he frequently repeats and rewords any difficult sentences or concepts.
- [How to Spanish Podcast](https://www.youtube.com/@HowtoSpanishPodcast_Official)
  - The pace of How to Spanish is a little faster than the content creators above, but David and Ana speak very clearly. I like the episodes about language learning and useful terms for specific situations (like going to the hair salon.)
- [Español a la Mexicana](https://open.spotify.com/show/4rqeShQpXXxwBC3EfraUbK?si=3ddd8091f5b648bd)
  - These episodes are super quick and easy to listen to while working around the house. The episodes are mostly about language learning and life in Mexico. I'd say the podcast is an easier intermediate and a nice way to dip your toes into audio-only CI.
- [Luisito Comunica](https://www.youtube.com/@luisitocomunica)
  - I had never heard of Luisito Comunica before learning Spanish with CI, but he's actually one of YouTube's biggest travel vloggers. He has a fun personality and his videos are a great transition to simpler "native" content. I find that travel vlogs, or anything with a show-and-tell format are still easy to follow along, even if you don't understand everything that's spoken. His series in Cuba is great, and my favorite video is inside the Cuban cigar factory.
- [Calixto Serna](https://www.youtube.com/@calixtoserna-mexicocooking5842)
  - Calixto is a travel food vlogger who was comprehensible to me around 400 hours. I like all of his demonstrations on regional Mexican cooking ([moles in Oaxaca](https://youtu.be/ffHCOfS19CQ?si=Za7PTHu_Xiw_O_Dr)) and his [videos](https://youtu.be/Zgy6tAzLa1c?si=WHQGtlHQhD7KEvlG) on eating in Basque Country were a great reference point for my trip.
